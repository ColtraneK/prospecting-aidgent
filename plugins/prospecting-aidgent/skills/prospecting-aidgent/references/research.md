# Find a worthwhile conversation

Adapted from First Customer Finder's evidence-led research approach; see THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md. Use its useful reasoning, not its old quantity caps or HTML delivery.

Read `research-framework.md` before the search. It is the preserved detailed framework: specific business brief, concrete query buckets, original-source evidence, five qualification dimensions and grounded outreach. Use it, rather than replacing the process with a generic “find relevant people” search. Its scores are research judgments, not calibrated buying probabilities. Apply the same evidence discipline to partnerships: the need can be a demonstrated complementary implementation gap, not only a pain the partner personally wants to buy a solution for.

Work in combined relationship + public-discovery mode unless the user asks otherwise. Public discovery is a continuing research track, not only a fallback after exhausting connections. Verify new candidates against the supplied network and active/archive records; absence from an export does not prove the user has never met them. Keep separate candidate hypotheses for customers and partners; do not silently turn the whole assignment into referral sourcing. Translate corrections into exclusions and search terms before continuing. For example, a firm 10-person maximum excludes larger companies and leaves unknown headcount unverified; a fractional title alone does not prove a one-person company. If local geography is a requirement, verify it rather than treating a relevant industry as “nearby.” These are examples, not defaults for every business.

First form a brief specific enough to reject weak matches: offer/outcome, customer versus partner, work the person actually does, buyer, geography, alternatives, exclusions and 3–6 observable signals. Honor the user's methodology and learn from their examples; do not hardcode an AI-agency ICP for everyone.

Search several independent signal buckets: explicit requests for help; operational pain; expensive workarounds; switching/pricing changes; and relevant hiring, launches, growth or process changes. Open original sources rather than treating search snippets as proof. For an AI agency, a job describing repeated CRM-to-spreadsheet copying can suggest an integration need; hiring an assistant alone does not prove the role should be automated. A fractional operator can be a referral partner when evidence shows a complementary implementation gap, not merely the right title.

For promising people, verify identity, current role and location where required. Seek a real post, article, announcement, job description or other substantive evidence that supports the opener. A LinkedIn profile establishes contact identity; it is not automatically the reason to contact them. An accessible official service page can support a partnership hypothesis, with no claim that it is fresh news. Dates unavailable stay unavailable; crawl times are not publication dates. Keep source publication dates distinct from upcoming event or release dates in drafts: say “your update about the report due October 8,” not “your October 8 update” when that is the future release date. Separate observations, inference and what remains uncertain.

Privately rank business fit, strength of evidenced need, timing, reachability and source reliability. Relationship warmth informs the opener, not invented fit. Strong specific fit with imperfect timing can support a clearly labeled useful question, rather than no result at all; do not include generic title-only names or ignore contrary evidence to reach a count. For stricter workspace rules, keep uncertain matches in private watch state and continue sourcing. Aim for 15+ useful recommendations across past conversations, relevant connections and new discoveries; use a flexible mix, not an arbitrary quota in each lane. Keep research coverage and an honest shortfall.

Every row should let the person decide and act: specific Fit, honest Why now, one useful tailored Message, direct evidence URL and verified personal LinkedIn URL when available. If LinkedIn cannot be verified, use another real professional contact path and explain the fallback in Context. Never guess email patterns. Keep H/I-equivalent fields as single raw clickable URLs; source explanation and additional links go in Context. Avoid irrelevant biography as evidence.

Before drafting, read the user's voice preferences and examples they confirm they wrote. Infer a provisional voice if no examples exist; let them correct it without another setup wizard. Default to warm, specific, peer-to-peer language: real context, a helpful observation/resource/question, a concise reason you can help, one low-pressure next step. Usually 40–90 words, no generic compliments or invented familiarity. A thoughtful comment or sharing a useful resource may be better than a meeting request. Do not force a comment without a real post or turn every partnership into a sales pitch. Do not copy an imported stranger's voice as the user's.

For large lists, batch research, save dispositions and source locators, deduplicate against Leads and Archive, and continue across runs only on a verified schedule or explicit follow-up. Stop at access/rate/budget constraints and report what remains. Never call 15 rows “all high-quality connections researched” if only a small subset was reviewed.

Before ending a first delivery, check the actual outcome: does the Sheet contain the supported recommendations, with drafts and useful URLs, or are there only local notes? If fewer than the target, have you investigated promising existing relationships and tried several public signal buckets, or merely stopped after the first small batch? Continue when safe, authorized research remains possible. If writing alone is blocked, still finish a useful research batch with complete per-person records so a later connector fix does not require researching them again.
