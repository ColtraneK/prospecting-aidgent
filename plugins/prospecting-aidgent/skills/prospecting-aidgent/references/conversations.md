# Prepare, listen, follow through

Use this for professional conversation preparation, a supplied interaction update, meeting debrief or review of commitments. Keep the existing Sheet and research workflow; no new tabs, relationship scores, mandatory forms or outbound capabilities. A quick question deserves a quick answer, not a dossier.

## A brief before a conversation

Use current authorized workspace context and permitted public sources. Resolve the right person by existing identity/contact information, not name alone. Read relevant recent user updates before older exports. Ask only if identity, context or the purpose materially changes the advice; otherwise make an inspectable provisional recommendation.

Default to roughly 250–400 words or shorter when evidence is limited:

- Their current work and the purpose of this conversation.
- What has changed or matters in their world, with sources and dates when available. Separate observed facts from possible relevance; do not call an undated service page recent news.
- Two natural questions rooted in the actual context, not a scripted interrogation.
- One genuinely useful contribution to consider, if supported: a resource, relevant observation, small example or introduction the user could choose to offer. No contribution is better than invented availability or a generic giveaway.
- One important uncertainty or boundary: what not to assume, pitch or repeat.

Honor a request for more depth, but do not pad weak evidence. Treat private exchanges as private source notes, not public links or Sheet transcripts. Do not infer birthdays, sensitive personal traits, closeness or motivations. An event, ticket, introduction or paid service is only an idea until availability, appropriateness and any necessary authorization are established. Do not book, buy, introduce, distribute assets or contact anyone.

## A debrief after a conversation

Accept rough dictation, notes or a short update without requiring a format. Treat transcription as potentially imperfect. Extract only what helps future professional action:

- Confirmed interaction and outcome, with source and event date if known.
- Explicit commitments: who agreed to do what, and by when only if supplied.
- Tentative ideas and unanswered questions, kept separate from commitments.
- One recommended next move and why, which can be waiting or no action.

“Maybe I could send a sample” is not a promise. “I sent the sample” is a user-reported completed action, not proof it was received or read. If wording conflicts or a date changes the action, ask one focused question rather than silently resolving it. Preserve the update and uncertainty while waiting; do not block unrelated research.

## Stale context and continuity

When the user says they have since called, texted or met someone, the prior outreach advice may be stale even if the outcome is unknown. Immediately record that caveat for this person and stop reusing the old opener. Ask what changed before proposing a follow-up dependent on the result. Do not erase historical evidence, mark a deal won, infer a reply or set a Sheet status on their behalf.

Keep concise derived notes under data/private/conversations/ in the current workspace, created only when needed. Use a stable existing person ID or an opaque ID linked to verified contact information; do not merge same-name people. Include source/provenance, recorded date, interaction date (or unknown), facts, tentative interpretations, confirmed commitments and superseded advice. Link the note and any pending update in state/progress.json so a fresh task can find it. Merge updates without discarding unrelated state. Use state/business.md only for relevant business-wide preferences, not all personal conversation details.

Retain minimal professional context; do not copy raw transcripts, unnecessary intimate details or sensitive personal material into the Sheet or plugin package. Do not turn one person's preferences into a rule about every prospect. Sheet research updates still follow references/sheets.md; Feedback, Status, custom cells and human-edited drafts remain protected. Private notes never justify overwriting a newer human decision.

## Optional inbox-informed relationship map

When supplied relationship history has gaps, explain the benefit of an inbox connection: distinguishing whom the user actually contacted, what was discussed, and whose next move is supported by the exchange. Offer it after useful progress or when asked, not as a mandatory setup step. Discover available email connectors and their actual read capabilities; use an existing authorized connection before proposing another provider or sign-in. Do not invent connection links, request passwords, promise access or treat this reference as permission to read email.

Agree a bounded starting scope, such as active leads and a relevant time range, before reading. Broad inbox mining or new recurring access requires explicit scope approval. If the connection is unavailable or declined, continue with the export, user updates and public research. Do not block prospecting or repeatedly prompt for connection.

Match people using verified addresses, domains and conversation context, not names alone. Separate sent messages, drafts (including self-addressed drafts), received replies, automated notices and newsletters. A sent message proves a recorded send, not that it was read; an event notification is not a personal relationship; no search match means unconfirmed, not never contacted. Check enough thread context to support who owes a next move, and leave it unknown when ambiguous. Newer user-provided text/call updates can supersede older email interpretations.

Record minimal professional summaries with source/time and uncertainty in private conversation notes, linked from progress. A useful map can simply group people by relationship/opportunity, last verified interaction, supported next move and missing context. It need not be a graph or another table. Show useful summaries in existing research fields only when appropriate; ask before adding agreed agent-owned columns. Do not dump raw emails, private message permalinks or unnecessary personal details into the Sheet. Never write human Status/Feedback to reflect email findings, mark messages read, label, archive, delete, draft inside the inbox or send mail. Stage message drafts locally or in authorized Sheet research fields.

## Supportive accountability

During an authorized review/check-in, bring forward relevant explicit commitments or the next action the user chose. An example is: “You planned to send the example; I don't have an update. Did that happen? I can help make a short version.” Do not use fixed scripts when a more natural response fits.

Help the user choose, complete, postpone or drop an action. Missing updates are unknowns, not failures. Avoid repeated reminders, pressure, guilt and unsolicited productivity scoring. Ask about accountability preferences when they become relevant, not during initial setup. Honor requests for less coaching. Use the existing approved check-in scope; reminders, schedule changes and proactive notifications still require their own authorization. Do not silently broaden a research-only routine to personal accountability.

Mirror their preferred tone and level of detail, not unsupported beliefs or manipulative tactics. Offer a concise, reasoned alternative when an approach is likely to be irrelevant or misleading. Giving something useful does not entitle the user to a reply. Draft messages around one honest purpose: share, ask, close a loop or propose a suitable next step. Use short messages by default when the user prefers them; do not pad to meet a word range or enforce that preference on every business.

## Keep discovery intact

Relationship depth complements new prospect discovery. A briefing request does not trigger a new research campaign; a sourcing request does not become a coaching session. Keep the user's chosen goals, volume and methodology unless they change them. A one-off five-person trial is not a permanent volume limit or a default for other users. Do not change an existing schedule's quantity without approval.
